# Addendum: business not run for profit

This business project is run as a non-profit: for example an NGO, a charity or foundation, an industry consortium establishing a shared standard, a public-interest research programme, or a community-run open-source project. It exists to deliver value to its members, beneficiaries or the public, not profit for its owners.

Where this prompt, its questions or its output fields refer to return on investment (ROI), revenue, profitability or financial returns, apply cost-effectiveness instead: the outcomes the project delivers per unit of money spent (e.g., cost per person served, per unit of risk reduced, per tonne avoided), how large and lasting those outcomes are, and how they compare with the alternatives. Money held back without a matching risk delivers no outcome, so size reserves to the risks they cover. Parts of the plan that do earn money (fees, offtake, sales) are still judged on revenue and ROI.

The project's core capability is its reason to exist; do not treat it as missing because no single commercial use-case dominates. Where adoption matters, assess it realistically: who would adopt, operate, maintain or fund the result, under what conditions, compared with which alternatives, and what stands in their way. Express the strategic objectives as outcomes and the cost per outcome where it can be measured.
