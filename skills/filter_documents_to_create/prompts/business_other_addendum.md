# Addendum: business not run for profit

This business project is not run for profit, and not as a non-profit either: it is, for example, a government programme, public infrastructure, an agreement between countries, or a public-private hybrid. No owner profits from it; it exists to deliver outcomes for the parties involved. Where parts of the plan earn money (fees, offtake, sales), their commercial viability still matters.

Rate revenue, investor, pitch or return-on-investment documents 'Low' unless the project description explicitly asks for them. Viability means technical viability, cost and funding feasibility, and whether the project delivers meaningful outcomes for the parties involved compared with alternatives (e.g., a Cost and Funding Feasibility Assessment rather than a Financial Feasibility Assessment). An Adopter and Beneficiary Needs Strategy takes the place of a Market Research Strategy, and funding agreements count as non-negotiable prerequisites.
